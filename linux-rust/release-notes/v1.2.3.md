# Kase 1.2.3

**Released:** 2026-11-06 · **Type:** patch release · **Breaking changes:** none

When the Linux Bluetooth service gets stuck and refuses to connect your AirPods, Kase now offers to restart it with one click and then reconnects them by itself. You no longer need a terminal.

## Added

- **One-click Restart Bluetooth when Bluetooth gets stuck.** Sometimes the AirPods drop the PC halfway through setting up audio. This often happens when your iPhone takes them back at the same moment. When it does, the Bluetooth service (BlueZ) can keep its audio part marked "still connecting" for good, and then refuse every connect, from Kase and from the desktop's Bluetooth menu alike. Kase 1.2.1 tries to clear this with a Disconnect. That works for one kind of jam but not this one, and neither does switching Bluetooth off and on. Only restarting the service clears it. So when Kase's clearing hasn't helped twice in a row (about 20 seconds), Kase now shows a **Bluetooth is stuck** notification with a **Restart Bluetooth** button. The button asks for your password, restarts the service, and Kase reconnects the AirPods within a few seconds. Other Bluetooth devices drop for a moment while the service restarts. Kase never restarts Bluetooth without asking. If your system can't show a password prompt, the notification gives the command to run instead. ([#73])

## How it was checked

**What the logs show (David's PC, 5 November):**

- The jam happened twice. Each time, the AirPods dropped the link during audio setup: `No reply to Open request` at 13:11:40 UTC, and `No reply to SetConfiguration request` at 14:23:54 UTC. After that, every connect failed with `br-connection-busy`.
- The second time, BlueZ logged the reason on every attempt: `a2dp-sink profile connect failed: Device or resource busy`. In the BlueZ 5.87 source, that comes from the audio sink's own "connect in progress" marker. That marker is only cleared by the setup's own callback, which never arrived, or when the service is freed.
- 1.2.1's Disconnect ran 12 times between 14:23:55 and 14:29:31 and didn't clear it. The desktop's Bluetooth off/on at 14:26 reset the adapter (the kernel re-initialised it) and didn't clear it either. `systemctl restart bluetooth` at 14:30:24 did. Kase reconnected the AirPods 2 seconds later and the audio was ready on AAC at 14:30:30, with no clicks needed.
- Kase 1.2.2 removes the most likely trigger: Kase taking the AirPods off the iPhone the moment they reconnect. This release covers the cases that still happen.

**Tests:** a new test checks that the restart is offered once, after two clears that didn't help, and not again for the same jam. Another checks that the helper's `restart` mode changes no configuration. All library tests pass, and clippy and rustfmt are clean.

**Not yet seen on real AirPods:** the notification button and the password prompt haven't run against a real jam, because it can't be triggered on demand. When it happens, the log says `offering to restart Bluetooth` and then `restarted Bluetooth`.

## Upgrading

Open a terminal and run:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DaBarkle/kase/main/linux-rust/packaging/install.sh | bash
```

Kase restarts in the tray with your settings, saved AirPods and Flatpak overrides intact.

## Known limitations

- This is a bug in BlueZ itself, and a draft report for the BlueZ project is ready. Until BlueZ fixes it, a restart is the only cure, so Kase asks for one.
- The notification waits up to 30 minutes for an answer. If you dismiss it, `sudo systemctl restart bluetooth` still works.
- Everything listed for [1.1.0](https://github.com/DaBarkle/kase/releases/tag/v1.1.0) still applies.
