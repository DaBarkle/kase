# Kase 1.2.1

**Released:** 2026-11-06 · **Type:** patch release · **Breaking changes:** none

Your AirPods connect by themselves after a connection drops halfway, instead of staying off the PC until you restart the Bluetooth service.

## Fixed

- **AirPods no longer stay off the PC after a connection drops halfway.** When you open the case, Kase connects at once. On 5 November that connection dropped two seconds later, while BlueZ was still setting up the audio. After that, BlueZ refused every connect as busy without trying the radio, so opening the lid or taking the pods out did nothing. The case card showed for a moment and went away because it only shows while connected. Only restarting the Bluetooth service got them back. Kase now recognises this and sends BlueZ an ordinary Disconnect, which needs no root, then connects again. It acts straight away when a cancelled connect is followed by a busy answer, and otherwise after 15 seconds of busy answers, which is longer than a real connect attempt takes. ([#70])
- **The log now says why each connection dropped.** Kase used to keep BlueZ's reason out of its normal log.

## How it was checked

**What the logs and the BlueZ source show:**

- The connect was cancelled at 13:11:40 UTC, in the same second as the only `No reply to Open request` from BlueZ since 28 October. It's also the only cancelled connect among the 8,822 connect results in Kase's logs. 20 busy refusals followed over three and a half minutes.
- The fault was inside the Bluetooth service, not the AirPods, an iPhone or the Bluetooth adapter. The refusals came back in the same second, with no radio attempt. Restarting the adapter at 13:15:02 didn't clear it, and the refusal at 13:15:05 proves that. Restarting the service at 13:15:35 did, and the AirPods connected two seconds later.
- BlueZ 5.87 has one source for this refusal: a check of three internal fields. The dropped link clears one of them and leaves the other two, and Disconnect clears all three.

**What isn't known yet:**

- **Which of the two remaining fields stuck, and the exact path in BlueZ that left it set.** Reading the source didn't settle it, and BlueZ's debug logging was off. So the root cause is known down to the stuck state in BlueZ, not to the line of BlueZ code at fault.
- **Why the connection dropped.** Kase didn't log the reason. It wasn't a Disconnect on the PC, since Kase kept retrying. Its log line saying another device took them is a guess. This version logs the reason.
- **Whether the fix works on real AirPods.** This has happened once and can't be triggered on demand, so the Disconnect hasn't run against a stuck BlueZ yet. When it does, the log says `BlueZ is stuck on an old connect`, and `Reconnected` should follow.

**Tests:** new tests cover when Kase decides BlueZ is stuck, how it reads BlueZ's errors, and that its own Disconnect is never mistaken for you pressing Disconnect. All library tests pass, and clippy and rustfmt are clean. `docs/TESTING.md` has a new row in the case and reconnect table.

## Upgrading

Open a terminal and run:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DaBarkle/kase/main/linux-rust/packaging/install.sh | bash
```

Kase restarts in the tray with your settings, saved AirPods and Flatpak overrides intact.

## Known limitations

- If the Disconnect doesn't clear BlueZ, Kase tries again every time the busy answers have gone on for 15 seconds, and `sudo systemctl restart bluetooth` is still the fallback.
- Everything listed for [1.1.0](https://github.com/DaBarkle/kase/releases/tag/v1.1.0) still applies.
